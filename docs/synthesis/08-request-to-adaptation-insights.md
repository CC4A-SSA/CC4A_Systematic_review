## What we ask for

1. **Coverage.** Which of our 13 practices (list below) their synthesis covers, for which crops or livestock systems and which countries.
2. **Stress/normal-separated effects.** For covered practices: effect sizes separated into stress-season and normal-season performance (g_stress, g_norm), with their source studies. This is our buffering coefficient; where they have it, we do not search for it.
3. **Their season classification.** The hazard benchmark they used to label stress seasons, and the climate dataset. We need to know whether their labels are comparable to our fixed Atlas thresholds, or whether we should reclassify from the studies' coordinates and dates.
4. **The evidence-tier rubric as they apply it**, and the M1–M4 context-matching hierarchy the indicator refers to but does not define in v3.0. We use the indicator's Tier 1–3 and want to apply it the same way.
5. **The reference-database schema** — fields and controlled values — so our recipe export maps onto it field for field.
6. **Unclassified studies they hold** — trials and evaluations with coordinates and dates but no season classification — for our Stream 2 register.
7. **Their provisional N** (minimum site-seasons, distinct stress events) if they have settled one. We use ≥ 2 distinct events as the entry condition and ≥ 3 for Tier 3, provisionally.

**Our practices:** AWD; biochar; rotational / planned grazing; conservation agriculture (reduced tillage + residue); cover crops; nutrient management (efficiency / ISFM); trees in farmlands (parklands, alley cropping); fodder banks; feed management (intensive and mixed); silvopasture; direct-seeded rice; SRI; enhanced rock weathering. Definitions in `01-practice-definitions.md` — send it with the request.

## What we offer

1. **Season classification.** We will classify the seasons of their unclassified studies against the Atlas hazard stack from coordinates and dates. The indicator is explicit that a rigorous evaluation with no season classification cannot be used, that a large stock of CGIAR studies is in that state, and that the fix is a desk exercise. We need the same classification for our own coefficients, so the work is shared rather than duplicated.
2. **Severity-stratified hazard probabilities** (moderate, severe, extreme) on the 5 arcmin grid across SSA, and the new SSA flood layer.
3. **Our extractions** for practices they do not cover, in their schema.
4. **Method feedback from a first application at scale**: the severity aggregation (their single stress class recovered exactly from our four), the transferability limit of a merged factor, and the cross-hazard co-occurrence problem.
