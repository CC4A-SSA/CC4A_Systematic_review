# 6137948 — Obiri et al. 2007, herbaceous legume fallows, Ghana — extraction notes

Extractor: Claude subagent (claude-opus-5-5), 2026-09-30. The CSVs are produced by `scratchpad/build_6137948.py` and checked by `scratchpad/check_6137948.py`. All verbatims are exact slices of the text dump.

## Practice assignment: cover_crops (not improved fallow)
- The species are all herbaceous legumes: Mucuna pruriens, Lablab purpureus, Stylosanthes spp., Pueraria phaseoloides and Canavalia spp. None is a tree or planted woody shrub. (Caveats: the authors call Canavalia "the shrub" with strong vines, Stylosanthes is sub-shrubby, Pueraria is perennial, and the Conclusions say "legume shrub fallow" twice.)
- Management: each legume is relayed into maize 5–8 weeks after the maize is sown (June–July). It then covers the soil through the dry season for about 8 months, and is cleared by hand (Feb–Mar or Mar–Apr) before the season-2 maize. This is one within-year cover phase between two maize crops, not a multi-year fallow. The legume is not harvested: seed "may be consumed" but is not valued (p. 9).
- The authors themselves say the terms "green manure" and "cover crops" can describe the technology (QTE-018). It fits 01 §2.5, "non-harvested plants grown in … relay with the main crop to cover the soil", and the species are in the §2.5 search string.
- 01 §3 excludes improved fallow (b60.x, a19.x) because rotational clearing makes above-ground **woody** carbon transient. That rationale is about shrub and tree fallows, so I did not apply it here. **Flag for the reviewer:** the authors frame the technology as a "fallow". If the team reads §3 literally, the whole paper moves out of scope.
- era_codes: `none; nearest b11.3, b12.3, b13.3 (relay green manure)`. I found no h code for a natural-fallow control, so comparator_era_code = none.
- Because the paper fits cover_crops, no gap row was written.

## contrast_type = isolated
- The legume subplots and the control sit inside the same farmer maize field, with the same maize variety, the same planting, and no fertiliser in any arm. The legume is the only difference.
- Caveat 1: the **cost budget** assumes the season-2 maize is weeded once after a legume fallow and twice after natural fallow. The paper does not say whether the yield plots were actually weeded differently, so I rated rob_comparator_fidelity = some.
- Caveat 2: the comparator is a natural (weedy) bush fallow, not a bare fallow. It is still the "same system without the cover crop".

## Arms and seasons
- Effects: 5 legume arms × 2 years (2001, 2002) = 10 rows, each against "Natural fallow", from Table 2 (kg/ha maize grain).
  - **2001 is the relay year**: the legume was growing under the maize. All five legume arms yielded less maize than the control (703–990 vs 1002 kg/ha). These are kept as unfavourable results.
  - **2002 is the maize after the fallow**: all arms yielded more than the control (1652–2204 vs 990).
  - The 2001 differences may reflect relay competition or baseline plot differences. The paper does not say which.
- finding_direction = mixed: negative or null in the relay year, positive in the following crop.
- The "Legume mean" row is not extracted as an effect, because it is an average of arms and would double-count. The same applies to the Table 6 "All legumes" column in costs.
- aggregation = pooled_sites. The yields are pooled over the 25 farms that repeated the trial in both seasons, across 3 villages, and no per-village yields are reported. site_season_id is therefore empty on the effect rows. Six site_season rows (3 villages × 2 years) are filled for S5.
- n_control = 25: all 25 analysed experiments had a control plot. This is inferred from QTE-007. n_treatment is left blank: each farmer grew only 2 of the 5 legumes and the per-species n is not reported. No dispersion statistics are reported.
- reported_condition / author_condition = unknown. The authors never describe a season as normal or dry.
  - Some farmers reported poor legume establishment "due to water-logging as a result of excessive rains" (p. 15). This is field-level, the year is unspecified, and it is not a season classification.
  - The 20 % yield-decline sensitivity ("should drought in the second season…") is hypothetical.
- Yield caveat: Table 2 is titled "values estimated for the analysis" and the text mentions "several informed assumptions". The yields were measured on 5 × 5 m plots, but I rated rob_outcome_measurement = some.
- rob_assignment = unclear. Farmers enrolled voluntarily, the paper does not say how species or subplots were assigned, and no randomisation is reported.
- rob_selective_reporting = some. 65 fields were planted, but only the 25 that repeated the trial on the same plots were analysed, and no per-village or per-farm results are given.
- rob_confounding = some. The 2001 control exceeds every legume arm.
- dose_farm_feasible = yes. The seed quantities are small, the species are local, and the authors call it simple, scale-neutral and low-input. However, the seed was supplied by researchers, and the extra labour falls in the lean season (June–July).
- meets_methodology_definition = unclear.
- years_since_establishment is left blank. It is not a core requirement for cover crops, and it differs by season (2001 = relay year, 2002 ≈ 1 year after sowing).
- water_regime = rainfed is inferred: no irrigation is mentioned, and the text describes the bimodal rainfall and says no inputs were used.

## Site-season
- **lat/lon are not reported. They are left blank and coord_source is left empty.** They need geocoding:
  - Gogoikrom, ~13 km from Nkawie and 48 km from Kumasi, Atwima district, Ashanti Region.
  - Subriso III, on the northern border of Tano district with Ashanti Region.
  - Yabraso, 19 km north-west of Wenchi, Brong Ahafo Region.
- Planting dates:
  - 2001: maize "between March and April", recorded as 2001-03.
  - 2002: maize "between February and March", recorded as 2002-02.
- Harvest dates:
  - 2002: "between August and September", recorded as 2002-08.
  - 2001: left blank. The text says only "about 12–16 weeks after planting". p. 8 also says yield was recorded "at harvest between August and September" without naming the season, which is ambiguous.
  - The dates conflict slightly: the relay text says the fallow was cleared Feb–Mar (p. 7), while the labour text says March/April (p. 8).
- Seasonal rainfall is not reported. Only district mean annual rainfall is given (Atwima 1400–1850 mm, Tano ~1500 mm, Wenchi 1140–1270 mm). This was not extracted because it is not seasonal.
- Soils are given only as soil classes (forest and savannah Ochrosols). No texture, pH or SOC is reported.

## Costs (65 rows)
- **Currency.** The paper uses "¢" (old Ghana cedi) with 2001–2002 prices. It was published in 2007, the year of the cedi redenomination (GHC → GHS at 10,000:1, July 2007). I recorded **GHC**, the pre-2007 ISO code. **Do not treat these values as GHS.**
- Nothing was converted. The Table 2 footnote gives £1 = ¢11,000 (2001) and ¢13,000 (2002); this is quoted only to identify the currency.
- Price year: prices are "average farm gate figures … in 2001 and 2002".
  - Season-1 items (legume planting, weeding, seed) use price_year 2001.
  - Season-2 items and the two-season totals use 2002.
  - All use price_year_basis = assumed_data_year. The totals mix 2001 and 2002 prices.
- unit_basis = per_ha throughout. The Table 2 header says "ha−1". Table 6 gives no denominator, but its values are consistent with per-ha figures, so per ha is an assumption for Table 6. Totals cover the 20-month, two-season cycle. There is no enum for "per ha per two seasons", so this is stated in the item text instead.
- What was extracted:
  - Table 2: total variable cost over two seasons, per arm (6 rows).
  - Table 6: the practice-relevant items per arm (35 legume rows + 4 natural-fallow rows): land preparation/clearing, weeding maize season 2, planting legume, weeding legume, legume seeds, total labour costs, total expenses. For the control, the zero-valued legume items were skipped.
  - Table 3: labour days, legume mean vs natural fallow (8 rows).
  - Table 4: total man-days per arm (6 rows).
  - Table 5: fallow-clearing man-days per arm, as average plus range (6 rows).
- **Not extracted from Table 6:** items that are identical across arms or driven by maize yield and marketing. These are planting maize, weeding season 1, harvesting, hauling, de-husking, shelling, bagging, land cost, machete, seed maize, storage, sacks, loading, tax and transport. They are system costs, not practice costs, but they are inside "Total expenses". Gross return and net cash flow were excluded by rule.
- Table 5 gives both an average and a range. I put the average in labour_days and the range in labour_days_min/max, which deviates slightly from the "range only → leave labour_days empty" rule.
- **Man-day = 5 hours** of hired labour (p. 9), not an 8-hour person-day. Chun should convert. The same note is in the item text of the Table 3 rows.
- The Table 3/4/5 labour-day rows have no money value, so currency, price_year and price_year_basis are left blank.
- labour_rate: the value is not stated. The paper says only "local daily wage rate", and that the by-day wage rose ¢1000 per year over 2000–2002.
- family_labour_costed = unstated. All man-days are priced at the wage rate, but the paper never says whether the budgeted labour is family or hired. Farm labour overall was 60 % family and 34 % hired.
- cost_class:
  - Legume planting, legume weeding and legume seed are establishment_fixed, following the authors' "establishment cost". They recur each cycle, so variable_running is also defensible.
  - Clearing and maize weeding are variable_running.
  - Reported totals are total, with author_computed = yes.
- cost_basis_source:
  - Clearing labour is trial_record: researcher and farmer estimates, verified by timing hired labour.
  - Everything else is expert_estimate: a budget built on "informed assumptions" and village-average prices, and the once-vs-twice weeding is an assumption.
- In-kind: maize and legume seed and technical backstopping were supplied by researchers in the trial, but costed in the budget as if the farmer paid. cost_bearer = farmer, following the budget perspective.
- Boundary quotes:
  - QTE-011: variable costs = establishment (seed + labour for sowing and one weeding), clearing, and weeding the next maize.
  - QTE-012: labour = man-days × daily wage; land rent charged.
  - QTE-013: weeding once vs twice.
  - QTE-015: man-day definition.
  - QTE-010: how clearing labour was measured.
  - QTE-038: the Table 6 other- and marketing-cost block, which is the boundary of total expenses.
- **Internal inconsistencies in the source (for the verifier):**
  - Table 2 total variable costs do not reconcile with the corresponding Table 6 items.
  - Table 3 clearing of legume fallow (7.0) differs from the Table 5 legume mean (7.3).
  - Table 3 legume total (45.4) differs from the Table 4 legume mean (46).
  - Table 6 gross return does not equal the Table 2 2001 + 2002 revenue.
  - All values were recorded as printed; nothing was recomputed.

## Not extracted by rule
NPV, IRR, returns to labour (Table 4 return columns), gross revenue, net cash flow, the sensitivity analyses (Tables 7–11), and maize prices.

## Adoption / perceptions (listed only; no adoption rows)
The paper reports no adoption or extent figures, so adoption_logged = false. The perception findings are:
- 65 farmers participated (43 men, 22 women); 69 participating and non-participating farmers took part in the indicator exercise.
- About 80 % rated the legumes better than natural fallow for soil fertility, crop yield, weed suppression and soil moisture. 18 % rated erosion control better.
- Mucuna vs Canavalia:
  - Mucuna better for soil fertility, weeds and moisture: ~75–80 %.
  - Crop yield: 50 % better, 33 % same, 17 % worse.
  - Labour: ~50 % better, 42 % worse.
  - Edibility: 75 % worse.
- Figures 2–4 are perception scores and were not extracted.
