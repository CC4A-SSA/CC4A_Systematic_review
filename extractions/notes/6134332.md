## Practice assignment and contrast
- **practice_id = biochar.** 10 t/ha rice-husk biochar, made in a retort kiln, applied as a field application in irrigated (flooded) lowland rice. Paddy rice goes to the biochar recipe per 01 §2.2, with water regime recorded (`irrigated_full`).
- **contrast_type = isolated.** The design is split-plot: biochar (0 vs 10 t/ha) is the main plot and N (0/45/90/120 kg N/ha) is the subplot. Each effect row compares biochar 10 with biochar 0 **at the same N rate**, with the same NPK and urea split, the same variety (KRC Baika) and the same flooding. This matches 01 §2.2: "co-applied with fertiliser where the control receives the same fertiliser". The N=0 rows compare biochar alone with no inputs, which is also isolated. I did **not** extract the confounded contrasts (e.g. B10+N120 vs B0+N0) because isolated contrasts exist for every cell.
- ERA codes: `b28; h10`, comparator `h10`. I took these from the practice definition and worked example and did not check them against era-aom.

## Arms and seasons included
- All 8 arms × 4 cropping cycles from Table 6. That gives 16 grain-yield rows (`crop_yield`) and 16 straw-yield rows (`biomass_yield`), each as a biochar-vs-no-biochar pair at N = 0, 45, 90 and 120.
- Negative results are kept. Straw yield under biochar was **lower** than without it in cycle 1 at N0 (3.70 vs 3.81), cycle 2 at N90 (5.23 vs 5.74) and N120 (6.56 vs 6.60), and cycle 4 at N90 (5.48 vs 5.68).
- Grain yield was numerically higher with biochar in all 16 cells. The abstract says "14 out of 16 instances"; the body and Table 6 do not explain the difference (possibly significance). finding_direction = positive (primary outcome grain), with a body quote.
- n = 3 per arm: "The subplots were replicated three times". Table 6 gives only mean-separation letters and p-values, so dispersion_type = none.
- Not extracted, because these are mechanism or out-of-scope outcomes: root volume (Table 5, cycles 1–2 only), grain filling %, N/P/K uptake (Figures 3–4, figure only), AEN/ARE (Table 7), end-of-trial soil properties and SOC stock (Table 8, Figure S2), GM/GR/VCR (Figures 5–6, Table S2), and phenology (Figure 2).

## Site-seasons
- 1 site (SIREC Kpong) × 4 cropping cycles = 4 site-seasons. Coordinates are reported as 6°09′ N, 00°04′ E, converted to 6.15, 0.0667 (the computation is in the quote row). coord_source = reported.
- Dates: planting is the transplanting month. Cycles 2 and 4 are given as ending "March/April". I used **April** (2013-04, 2014-04) on the strength of the Figure 1 caption, "spanned the period December to April". year = harvest year (2012, 2013, 2013, 2014).
- **author_condition (judgement call):**
  - Cycles 2 and 4 = `stress`. The authors say yields were lower "due to higher temperatures (particularly during the reproductive stage)" and point to >34 °C at flowering, so this is a heat description by the authors.
  - Cycles 1 and 3 = `normal`. The authors describe these only as cooler than cycles 2 and 4 and never call them normal or stressed, so `unknown` would also be defensible.
  - This is not a classification; S5 should test it with the heat hazard.
- Soil values are from Table 3, 0–15 cm, before the trial: pH 6.90, OC 6.7 g/kg (converted to 0.67 %), clay 580 g/kg (converted to 58 %). The AEZ is reported as "coastal savannah". No seasonal rainfall or irrigation supply was reported (the crop was irrigated and flooded).

## Study fields
- management = on_station, design = experiment_on_station (SIREC research station).
- rob_assignment = some, rob_confounding = some. N subplots were "completely randomized in each main plot", but the paper does not say how many biochar main plots there were or how they were randomised. Biochar may be unreplicated at main-plot level, which raises a pseudo-replication risk.
- rob_selective_reporting = low: all cycles and N rates are reported for grain and straw.
- years_since_establishment = "0-1.5". The single application was before cycle 1 (July 2012) and the last harvest was about April 2014. This is my approximation from the cycle dates; the paper states no number.
- dose_farm_feasible = unclear. At the reported 39 % char yield, 10 t/ha needs roughly 25 t of husk per ha (my arithmetic, not extracted). SIREC burns about 40 t of husk per cycle and the authors say husk is abundant near mills, but a smallholder's own residue would not supply it. meets_methodology_definition = unclear.
- water_control_level is left blank because it is not stated.

## Costs (Table 1, US$ per cycle, 2012–2014)
- There is no per-arm cost table in the main text (Table S2 is supplementary and not in the PDF).
  - The biochar-arm-only items are CST-001 (biochar preparation, US$17 per tonne) and CST-002 (biochar application labour, US$21/ha).
  - The 13 items common to both arms each got **one** row labelled "Both arms (with and without biochar)", rather than a duplicate row per arm. CST-006 (fertilizer application labour) applies only at N > 0.
- **cost_class (judgement call):** The authors classify biochar cost and fertiliser application as "variable" and the rest as "fixed (over the short term)". I used CC4A semantics instead:
  - Biochar preparation and application were one-off (applied once, cost in cycle 1: "higher production cost associated with applying biochar in the 1st cropping cycle"), so they are `establishment_fixed`, project_year = 1, and CST-002 is `per_ha` rather than per season.
  - Recurring per-cycle items are `variable_running`, although the authors call some of them fixed.
  - Chun may prefer the authors' split.
- CST-001 unit: the table says "Biochar preparation (tonne−1)" without saying whether that means per tonne of biochar or of feedstock. I assumed per tonne of biochar and set unit_basis = `per_tonne_input`, the closest enum value. Its boundary comes from the note under Table 1: labour for conveying and preparing the feedstock plus retort kiln rental. So labour = yes, transport = yes, equipment = hire, inputs = no (no feedstock price), and opportunity cost = no.
- price_year = "2012-2014", basis `stated` (Table 1 title). Currency is USD as reported; the authors converted at GHS 2.402/US$, which I recorded in source.note and did not use.
- Excluded, per the rules: fertiliser unit prices (NPK US$0.37/kg, urea US$0.31/kg), the selling price of rice (US$430/t), and GM/GR/VCR.
- No labour days are reported. The cost basis is `trial_record` ("obtained from the study site by the authors"). cost_bearer and family labour are unstated.
